1) Purpose and Scope

This report combines the strategic market analysis and the verification-focused findings for entry-level CDL options in Phoenix/Chandler where candidates can start with:

  • $0 upfront cost, or
  • company-financed training tied to employment.

Companies covered (all requested): Roehl, Knight, Swift, Schneider, Eagle, Old Dominion Freight Line (ODFL), XPO Logistics, Saia.

This updated version includes specific dollar amounts, pay rates, program durations, Arizona state CDL fees, and verified details sourced from official company websites, state DOT pages, and established trucking industry resources.

2) Executive Summary

  • The market splits into two models: immediate CDL (company-sponsored school, 2-8 weeks to CDL) vs. dock-to-driver (start as dock worker at $18-$28/hr, transition to driving months later).
  • Immediate CDL programs typically lead to OTR first-seat jobs earning $50,000-$70,000+ in year one, but carry contract lock-in of 9-15 months and early-exit penalties of $3,000-$7,000.
  • Dock-to-driver paths (ODFL, XPO, Saia) provide better first-job quality (local routes, home daily) and often higher long-term pay, but require patience and internal promotion.
  • Arizona CDL-A license fees are low ($25 for the license + $25 for the skills test), but all first-time CDL applicants must complete ELDT-compliant training through an FMCSA-registered provider.
  • Hair follicle testing is common among large carriers (Knight, Swift, Schneider, Eagle) in addition to the standard DOT urine test, creating a stricter eligibility filter.
  • Phoenix is a major freight hub with strong hiring from all 8 carriers. Knight is headquartered in Phoenix, and Swift has a large Phoenix academy and terminal presence.
Decision rule: Treat written terms (program docs, recruiter email, offer letter) as authoritative over verbal claims. Request the tuition agreement and repayment schedule in writing before committing.

3) Arizona CDL Process and Costs

Before entering any training program, it helps to understand the Arizona-specific CDL process and costs. Most sponsored programs handle the training, but candidates typically need a Commercial Learner's Permit (CLP) before starting.

Step 1: Get a DOT Medical Card

  • Required before applying for CLP. Must be performed by a medical examiner on the FMCSA National Registry.
  • Typical cost: $75-$150 at urgent care clinics or occupational health offices in the Phoenix area. Some company-sponsored programs cover this cost.
  • Valid for 24 months (or less, depending on medical conditions).
  • As of June 23, 2025, medical examiners submit results electronically to FMCSA, which forwards them to Arizona MVD. Drivers no longer need to submit their own Medical Examiner Certificates.

Source: AZDOT - Medical Certification

Step 2: Obtain a Commercial Learner's Permit (CLP)

  • Minimum age: 21 for interstate CDL (18 for intrastate-only, Arizona only).
  • Required documents: valid AZ driver's license, Social Security card, proof of authorized presence in the U.S., DOT medical card.
  • Pass the CDL knowledge tests (General Knowledge + combination vehicles for Class A). Minimum passing score: 80%.
  • Arizona CLP fee: $25 for Class A.
  • CLP is valid for 12 months.
  • You must hold the CLP for at least 14 days before taking the skills test (federal FMCSA requirement).

Source: AZDOT - CDL Instruction Permit

Step 3: Complete ELDT Training

  • Since February 7, 2022, all first-time CDL applicants must complete Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) from an FMCSA-registered training provider.
  • ELDT covers theory, range (behind-the-wheel), and road instruction.
  • All 8 companies in this report use ELDT-compliant programs or partner schools.

Step 4: Pass the CDL Skills Test

  • Three parts: vehicle inspection (pre-trip), basic controls (backing, docking), and road test.
  • Arizona skills test fee at MVD: $25 for Class A.
  • Can also be taken through MVD-certified third-party testers (many company programs use in-house testers).
  • If testing in an automatic-only vehicle, your CDL will carry a restriction to automatic transmission only.

Step 5: Receive Your CDL

  • Arizona CDL Class A license fee: $25.
  • Add $10 per endorsement (hazmat, tanker, doubles/triples, passenger).
  • Arizona CDLs are valid for up to 8 years.

Total Out-of-Pocket Cost (if self-pay)

Arizona CDL state fees alone: approximately $75-$85 (CLP $25 + skills test $25 + CDL $25 + endorsements if desired).
DOT physical: $75-$150.
CDL school tuition (private): $3,000-$10,000 depending on school and program length.
Company-sponsored route: $0 upfront (company covers tuition; candidate pays only CLP knowledge test fee in most cases).

Source: AZDOT - CDL Fees

5) Phoenix/Chandler Market Structure

Immediate CDL Track (fast path)

  • Time to CDL: 2-8 weeks depending on program (school phase + on-the-road training phase).
  • Entry: No CDL required. Most require a CLP before starting (some help you obtain it).
  • Pay during training: Ranges from $400-$500/week during school phase up to $700-$900/week during OTR training phase.
  • Financial model: Forgivable tuition ($3,000-$7,000), payroll deductions (~$60/week from checks), or time/mileage commitment (9-15 months).
  • First seat: Usually OTR. Expect 2-4 weeks out at a time. First-year income typically $50,000-$70,000+.
  • Drug testing: Most major carriers conduct both DOT-mandated urine tests AND hair follicle tests. Hair testing detects usage over ~90 days vs. ~72 hours for urine.

Dock-to-Driver Track (delayed path)

  • Time to CDL: Months to 1+ year (dependent on internal promotion windows and training slot availability).
  • Entry: Dock/warehouse role first. Typical starting dock pay: $18-$28/hr depending on carrier and location.
  • Financial model: Generally no repayment obligation. XPO's driver school is explicitly tuition-free (a ~$10,000 value). Saia offers a $5,000 incentive-based dock-to-driver program.
  • First seat after promotion: Often local/regional (LTL). Home daily in many cases. ODFL reports 95% of drivers home daily.
  • Long-term pay: LTL drivers can earn $60,000-$80,000+ with experience. ODFL and Saia driver averages trend above OTR industry averages.

Phoenix Market Context

  • Phoenix is a top-10 US freight hub due to rapid population growth, proximity to Southern California ports, and major distribution corridors (I-10, I-17, I-40).
  • Knight-Swift (parent company of both Knight and Swift) is headquartered in Phoenix at 2002 W Camelback Road, making Phoenix their most active hiring market.
  • Average CDL-A truck driver salary in Phoenix: $52,122/year (Salary.com, April 2025), with a typical range of $45,591-$60,283. Experienced OTR drivers at dedicated carriers can reach $90,000-$100,000+.

8) Eligibility and Disqualifier Checklist

Universal Requirements

  • Age: 21+ for interstate CDL (required for all OTR and most LTL positions). Arizona allows 18+ for intrastate only.
  • Driver's license: Valid U.S. driver's license. Schneider requires at least 12 months of license history.
  • MVR (Motor Vehicle Record): Clean or acceptable driving record. Specifics vary - most carriers look back 3-5 years for moving violations.
  • DUI/DWI: Most carriers disqualify for any DUI/DWI within the past 5-7 years. Some have lifetime disqualifiers for multiple offenses.
  • DOT physical: Must pass and maintain current DOT medical certification. Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes, certain heart conditions, or sleep apnea may require additional documentation.
  • Drug testing: All CDL holders are subject to DOT-mandated urine testing. Many carriers (Knight, Swift, Schneider, Eagle) also perform hair follicle testing which detects substances over ~90 days. This is a stricter standard than urine (~72 hours detection).
  • Criminal background: Lookback period and disqualifying offenses vary by carrier. Felony convictions (especially recent ones) can be disqualifying. Each carrier has its own policy.
  • Work authorization: Must be legally authorized to work in the U.S.

CDL-Specific Requirements (Arizona)

  • CLP first: Must obtain CLP before entering any training program. Knowledge tests must be passed at 80%+ score.
  • ELDT compliance: All first-time CDL applicants must complete training from an FMCSA-registered ELDT provider.
  • 14-day hold: Must hold CLP for at least 14 days before skills test.
  • Automatic vs. manual: If you train and test on an automatic-only truck, your CDL will have a restriction to automatic transmissions. Most company programs train on automatic, but confirm if this matters for your career goals.
  • Endorsements: LTL carriers (ODFL, XPO, Saia) typically require Hazmat (H), Tanker (N), and sometimes Doubles/Triples (T) endorsements. Each endorsement adds $10 to your Arizona CDL fee and requires passing an additional knowledge test. Hazmat requires a TSA background check.

Personal Readiness Checklist

  • Can you tolerate OTR schedule (2-4 weeks away from home) for the first 6-12 months?
  • Can you absorb lower pay (~$400-$500/week) during the school/mentor phase?
  • Do you have emergency savings to cover the transition period?
  • Do you fully understand the repayment terms if you leave early?
  • Is your target job type (OTR vs. local/regional) aligned with the program you're choosing?

9) Financial Risk Checklist

Collect these in writing before committing to any program:

Question Why It Matters
What is the full tuition/training cost amount? Ranges from $0 (XPO, Schneider CAT) to ~$5,000-$7,000 (Roehl, Swift). Know the exact number.
Is the cost waived, reimbursed, deducted, or forgiven? Determines whether you owe money and how it's collected. Payroll deduction (Swift ~$60/wk) vs. forgiveness over time (Roehl 15 months) are very different.
What events trigger repayment? (quit, terminated, fail out, safety event) Being fired may still trigger repayment at some carriers. Get the exact trigger language.
What is the liability at month 1, 3, 6, and 12? Roehl's $5,000 is prorated over 15 months. Swift's deduction reduces over time. Get the schedule.
How is repayment collected and under what deadline? Some companies send to collections. Others deduct from final paycheck. Know your exposure.
What happens if I'm terminated for reasons beyond my control? Layoffs, medical issues, or family emergencies - does the contract have hardship provisions?
Rule of thumb: If a recruiter can't or won't provide the repayment terms in writing, that is a red flag. Walk away or escalate to a different recruiter.

11) Practical Decision Framework

If your priority is speed to CDL (fastest possible start)

  • Best options: Knight (2-3 weeks school), Roehl (3 weeks school), Swift (3-4 weeks school).
  • Trade-off: OTR first seat, 2-4 weeks away from home, contract lock-in of 9-15 months.
  • Expected first-year income: $50,000-$70,000+ depending on carrier and efficiency.

If your priority is best training quality

  • Best options: Schneider CAT (5-7.5 weeks, structured apprenticeship with simulators), Roehl (recognized for training quality).
  • Trade-off: Slightly longer before you're solo, but better preparation and potentially fewer early-career incidents.

If your priority is first-job quality and being home

  • Best options: XPO (7-week tuition-free school, LTL local routes, home every shift), ODFL (95% home daily), Saia (city drivers home daily).
  • Trade-off: Longer path to CDL (weeks to months). Dock work first at $18-$28/hr. But significantly better quality of life from day 1 as a driver.
  • Expected driver income: $50,000-$84,000+ depending on role and experience.

If your priority is lowest financial risk

  • Best options: XPO ($0 tuition, no contract, ~$10k training value free), ODFL (no formal repayment), Saia ($5k incentive program, no contract).
  • Avoid: Any program where repayment terms are unclear or only communicated verbally.
  • Among immediate-CDL carriers: Schneider CAT has the shortest reported commitment (9 months vs. Roehl's 15 months).

If your priority is highest first-year earning potential

  • Best options: Roehl ($70k+ claim with Dynamic Pay Plans), Schneider ($61,864 average first-year), Swift (top 25% earn $70,500 first year).
  • Note: First-year income is heavily dependent on miles driven and efficiency. The first 3-6 months will be lower as you ramp up.

12) Standard Verification Script (Use for All 8)

Ask each recruiter these exact questions and request written confirmation (email or document) for every answer:

  1. What is the exact CDL path from day 1 to solo dispatch? How many weeks/months total?
  2. What pay do I receive during school phase? During trainer/finishing weeks? Provide exact weekly amounts.
  3. What is the exact tuition/training cost? Is it waived, reimbursed, deducted from paychecks, or forgiven over time?
  4. What amount would I owe if I leave at 3, 6, and 12 months? What triggers repayment (quit, termination, medical, etc.)?
  5. For Phoenix hires specifically, where is orientation? Where will I be dispatched from? Is training local or do I need to travel?
  6. What first-seat type should I expect (OTR/regional/local)? What is expected home time in weeks?
  7. Is training/testing on automatic or manual? Will I have an automatic-only restriction on my CDL?
  8. Do you conduct hair follicle testing in addition to DOT urine testing? What is the lookback period?
  9. Is there a current class waitlist for Phoenix candidates? When is the next available start date?
  10. Can you send the full tuition agreement, repayment schedule, and employment contract in writing before I commit?
