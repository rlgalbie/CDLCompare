# CDL Company-Sponsored Training – Phoenix/Chandler Market Analysis

## Scope

Covers companies offering:

* $0 upfront CDL training OR company-financed training
* Repayment via employment (contract, payroll deduction, or reimbursement)
* Entry-level (no CDL required)

Companies analyzed:

* Roehl Transport
* Knight Transportation
* Swift Transportation
* Schneider
* Eagle Transportation
* Old Dominion Freight Line
* XPO Logistics
* Saia

---

## Section 1 — Program Structure Comparison

### Roehl Transport

* Training model: employee from day 1
* CDL duration: ~3 weeks
* Paid during CDL: yes
* Pay during training: ~$500–$700/week equivalent
* Repayment: time commitment (~120k miles / ~1 year)
* Upfront cost: $0
* Failure penalty: yes (training cost if quit early)

### Knight Transportation

* Training model: school + paid OTR training
* CDL duration: ~3–4 weeks school + 4–6 weeks OTR training
* Paid during CDL: sometimes no (school phase), yes during OTR
* Pay during training: ~$700–$850/week (OTR phase)
* Repayment: reimbursement / sometimes informal commitment
* Upfront cost: $0 or financed
* Failure penalty: moderate (depends on structure)

### Swift Transportation

* Training model: CDL academy + employment
* CDL duration: ~3–4 weeks
* Paid during CDL: limited or none during school
* Pay during training: varies (~$600–$900/week after CDL)
* Repayment: payroll deduction OR forgiveness over time
* Upfront cost: $0 (financed internally)
* Failure penalty: yes (remaining balance)

### Schneider

* Training model: apprenticeship (CAT program)
* CDL duration: ~5–7.5 weeks
* Paid during CDL: yes
* Pay during training: ~$500–$700/week
* Repayment: commitment OR reimbursement structure
* Upfront cost: $0
* Failure penalty: yes

### Eagle Transportation

* Training model: pre-hire + short CDL program
* CDL duration: ~2–3 weeks
* Paid during CDL: typically no
* Pay during training: minimal or none
* Repayment: employment tie
* Upfront cost: $0 down
* Failure penalty: yes

### Old Dominion / XPO / Saia (Dock-to-Driver model)

* Training model: warehouse/dock → CDL sponsorship
* CDL duration: varies (after internal promotion)
* Paid during CDL: yes (as employee)
* Pay during training: standard hourly dock pay (~$20–$30/hr typical)
* Repayment: none or minimal
* Upfront cost: $0
* Failure penalty: none in most cases

## Section 2 — Time to CDL + Job Readiness

Fastest path (3–4 weeks):

* Roehl
* Swift
* Knight
* Eagle

Moderate (5–8 weeks):

* Schneider

Slowest (3–12+ months):

* Old Dominion
* XPO
* Saia

**Insight:**

* Fast programs prioritize speed over job quality
* Slow programs prioritize job quality over speed

---

## Section 3 — First Job Outcome (Critical Differentiator)

### Roehl / Knight / Swift / Schneider / Eagle

* First job type: OTR (over-the-road)
* Home time: 2–4 weeks out typical
* Weekly miles: 2,000–3,000
* First year income: ~$45k–$65k

### Old Dominion / XPO / Saia

* First CDL job: local or regional (after promotion)
* Home time: daily or frequent
* Weekly miles: lower, but hourly pay
* First year income: ~$60k–$80k (after CDL transition)

**Insight:**

* OTR path = faster CDL, lower quality of life initially
* Dock-to-driver = slower CDL, significantly better first job

## Section 4 — Financial Model Breakdown

### Model A: Hard Contract

* Companies: Roehl, some Schneider cases
* Obligation: fixed time/miles
* Early exit cost: ~$5k–$8k
* Risk: high lock-in

### Model B: Payroll Deduction

* Companies: Swift, some Knight
* Obligation: declining balance
* Early exit cost: remaining balance
* Risk: medium

### Model C: Reimbursement

* Companies: Schneider, some Knight
* Obligation: stay long enough to earn back cost
* Early exit cost: opportunity loss
* Risk: lower

### Model D: Internal Promotion

* Companies: Old Dominion, XPO, Saia
* Obligation: none formal
* Early exit cost: none
* Risk: lowest

## Section 7 — Core Strategic Tradeoff

### Strategy 1: Immediate CDL

* Companies: Roehl, Knight, Swift, Schneider, Eagle
* Time to CDL: minimal
* Financial barrier: none
* Constraint: OTR + contract
* Flexibility: low

### Strategy 2: Delayed CDL

* Companies: Old Dominion, XPO, Saia
* Time to CDL: long
* Financial barrier: none
* Constraint: slower progression
* Flexibility: high
* Outcome quality: higher

---

## Section 8 — Decision Variables

1. Time horizon

    * Need CDL immediately → Tier 1 companies
    * Willing to wait → Dock-to-driver

2. Job type preference

    * Accept OTR → all fast programs
    * Want local → dock-to-driver is superior

3. Risk tolerance

    * Okay with contract → Roehl / Schneider
    * Prefer flexibility → Knight / Swift / LTL

4. Income trajectory

    * Faster start, lower ceiling initially → OTR path
    * Slower start, higher early stability → LTL path

## Section 10 — Applicant Eligibility + Disqualifier Checklist

> This section captures what can block entry before someone commits time.

### Universal Entry Filters (Most Carriers)

* Valid U.S. driver's license and acceptable motor vehicle record (MVR)
* Ability to obtain/hold DOT medical certification
* Background screening (scope and lookback vary by carrier/policy)
* Drug/alcohol screening policy compliance
* Work authorization and identity documentation
* Ability to meet training travel/scheduling requirements

### CDL Training-Specific Checks

* ELDT-compliant training path for first-time Class A applicants (federal requirement)
* CDL permit timing requirements (varies by state process)
* Skills test scheduling availability (local capacity can delay completion)
* Automatic-only training may create an automatic transmission restriction if not tested in manual (confirm per program)

### Company-Policy Variables to Confirm (Not Standardized)

* Age minimum (and differences for interstate routes)
* Hair test vs urine-only screening policy
* Prior convictions/incidents lookback rules
* Preventable accident thresholds
* Prior employment verification strictness
* Rehire policy and previous carrier history constraints

### Personal Readiness Checklist

* Can I tolerate OTR schedule for 6-12 months?
* Can I absorb low/variable pay during school phase if needed?
* Do I have emergency savings for transition weeks?
* Do I understand exact repayment terms if I exit early?
* Is this path aligned with my target job type (OTR vs local)?

## Section 11 — Recruiter Call Script + Verification Log

> Use the same script for every company so comparisons are apples-to-apples.

### Call Script (Ask Verbatim)

1. What is the exact CDL training path from day 1 to solo dispatch?
2. Am I paid during school, and at what exact rate?
3. What is pay during trainer/finishing weeks, and for how many weeks?
4. Is there a contract, payroll deduction, or reimbursement model?
5. What is the exact early-exit financial obligation at month 1, 3, 6, and 12?
6. What first-seat job will I actually be placed into from Phoenix/Chandler?
7. What is expected home time in that first seat?
8. Is freight mostly no-touch, drop/hook, or live unload?
9. Do you train/test on automatic, manual, or both?
10. What disqualifies candidates most often in this program?
11. Where is orientation and who pays travel/hotel/meals?
12. Can you send all terms in writing before I commit?
